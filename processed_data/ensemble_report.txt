Models fused: Traditional ML, CNN, Transfer Learning (YAMNet)
Fusion strategy: weighted averaging (Traditional ML=0.30, CNN=0.60, Transfer Learning (YAMNet)=0.10)

Strategy selection method: nested 5-fold cross-validation over the validation set (134
samples), NOT a single fit-and-score-on-the-same-split comparison. A single-split
comparison had shown stacking edging out weighted-averaging by 0.67 val macro-F1 points
(0.8727 vs 0.8660) -- nested CV showed this gap is noise: weighted-averaging mean
holdout macro-F1=0.8585 (std 0.0717) vs stacking mean=0.8569 (std 0.0699), stacking only
won 3 of 5 folds. The weight VALUES themselves were stable though -- 4 of 5 folds
independently converged on the same (0.30, 0.60, 0.10) weights. Given the tie in
generalization and stacking's much larger parameter count (~30 vs 2 free parameters,
more overfit-prone on 134 samples), weighted-averaging was chosen as the safer,
equally-accurate, more robust option. It also reduces YAMNet's influence on
disagreement cases relative to stacking, which is directly relevant: real production
logs on 2026-08-10 showed belt clips flipping to "brake" under the stacking config
when YAMNet was overconfident and wrong.
